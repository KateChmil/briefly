Cell Biology — Lecture Notes (Week 1-3)

Cell theory: all living things are composed of cells; the cell is the basic
unit of life; all cells arise from pre-existing cells.

Prokaryotic vs eukaryotic cells: prokaryotes lack a nucleus and membrane-bound
organelles (bacteria, archaea). Eukaryotes have a nucleus and organelles.

Key organelles:
- Nucleus: stores DNA, site of transcription.
- Mitochondria: ATP production via cellular respiration (Krebs cycle,
  oxidative phosphorylation). Inner membrane folds = cristae.
- Ribosomes: protein synthesis (free in cytoplasm or bound to rough ER).
- Endoplasmic reticulum: rough ER (ribosomes, protein folding) and smooth ER
  (lipid synthesis, detoxification).
- Golgi apparatus: modifies, sorts and packages proteins into vesicles.
- Lysosomes: digestion of macromolecules via hydrolytic enzymes (low pH).
- Chloroplasts (plants): photosynthesis; thylakoids contain chlorophyll;
  light reactions in thylakoid membrane, Calvin cycle in stroma.

Membrane structure: phospholipid bilayer with embedded proteins
(fluid mosaic model). Transport: passive (diffusion, osmosis, facilitated)
vs active transport (requires ATP, e.g. Na+/K+ pump). Endocytosis and
exocytosis move bulk material.

Cell cycle: G1, S (DNA replication), G2, M (mitosis + cytokinesis).
Mitosis phases: prophase, metaphase, anaphase, telophase.
Checkpoints regulate progression; cyclins and CDKs are key regulators.
