Cell Structure - Lecture Notes

Cell theory: all living things are made of cells, the cell is the basic
unit of life, and new cells come from existing cells.

Prokaryotes (bacteria, archaea) have no nucleus or membrane-bound
organelles. Eukaryotes keep their DNA in a nucleus and compartmentalise
chemistry in organelles.

Key organelles:
- Nucleus: stores DNA; transcription happens here.
- Mitochondria: ATP from cellular respiration; inner folds = cristae.
- Ribosomes: protein synthesis, free or bound to the rough ER.
- Rough ER: protein folding; smooth ER: lipids and detox.
- Golgi apparatus: sorts and ships proteins in vesicles.
- Lysosomes: break down macromolecules at low pH.
- Chloroplasts (plants): photosynthesis in thylakoids and stroma.

Membranes are phospholipid bilayers with embedded proteins. Passive
transport (diffusion, osmosis) needs no ATP; active transport (Na+/K+
pump) does.

Cell cycle: G1, S (DNA replication), G2, M (mitosis). Checkpoints with
cyclins and CDKs keep division in order.
